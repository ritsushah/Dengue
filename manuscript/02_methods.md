# 2. METHODS

This systematic review and meta-analysis of diagnostic test accuracy (DTA) was designed, conducted, and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for a Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Diagnostic Test Accuracy Studies (PRISMA-DTA) statement [1], the PRISMA-DTA Explanation and Elaboration document [2], the PRISMA 2020 statement [3], and the PRISMA-S extension for literature searches [4]. Methodological decisions follow Cochrane DTA guidance [5]. This section maps to PRISMA-DTA items 5–16 and 23–24.

## 2.2 Review Question and PIRD Framework

The review question was structured using the Population, Index test, Reference standard, Diagnosis of interest (PIRD) framework recommended for DTA reviews [5]:

> **What is the diagnostic accuracy of fluorescence immunoassay (FIA) compared with enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for the detection of dengue virus non-structural protein 1 (NS1) antigen in patients with suspected acute dengue infection?**

| PIRD Component | Operational Definition |
|----------------|------------------------|
| **Population (P)** | Patients of any age with clinically suspected acute dengue infection (fever duration ≤7 days from symptom onset), presenting to any healthcare setting in dengue-endemic or epidemic regions. Eligible specimens: venous whole blood, serum, or plasma. |
| **Index test (I)** | Fluorescence immunoassay (FIA) for dengue NS1 antigen, any commercially available platform (e.g., STANDARD F Dengue NS1 Ag FIA, SD Biosensor; NS1 Ag QUANTI CARD; or equivalent fluorescence-based reader-interpreted assays). Positivity defined by the manufacturer-recommended Cutoff Index (COI). |
| **Reference standard (R)** | Hierarchical: (Tier 1) reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for DENV RNA; (Tier 2) virus isolation ± RT-PCR; (Tier 3) composite reference (RT-PCR + paired IgM/IgG seroconversion ± NS1 ELISA); (Tier 4) NS1 ELISA alone when a molecular standard is unavailable. ELISA also functions as the **comparative test** in head-to-head analyses. |
| **Diagnosis / target condition (D)** | Acute dengue virus infection (DENV serotypes 1–4) confirmed by NS1 antigenemia and/or virological evidence during the acute febrile window. |

Secondary questions address whether FIA accuracy differs by (i) primary versus secondary infection, (ii) DENV serotype, (iii) timing of specimen collection (days 0–3 vs. 4–7), and (iv) commercial FIA platform.

## 2.3 Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility criteria were specified a priori and applied independently at title/abstract and full-text stages.

### 2.3.1 Inclusion Criteria

**Population**
- Patients of any age (neonates, children, adolescents, adults) with clinically suspected acute dengue.
- Fever duration ≤7 days from symptom onset at the time of specimen collection, either as an explicit inclusion criterion or as a documented mean/median fever duration ≤7 days.
- Presentation to inpatient, outpatient, emergency, primary-care, or sentinel-surveillance settings in dengue-endemic or epidemic regions.
- Specimen types: venous whole blood, serum, or plasma.

**Index test**
- FIA for dengue NS1 antigen, interpreted by an automated or semi-automated fluorescence reader (objective COI readout).
- Manufacturer-recommended or pre-specified positivity threshold.

**Reference standard / comparator**
- At least one of: RT-PCR, virus isolation, composite laboratory algorithm, or NS1 ELISA.
- Studies evaluating FIA against ELISA, FIA against RT-PCR, or both tests against a common independent reference were eligible.

**Study design and data**
- Cross-sectional DTA studies; prospective or retrospective diagnostic cohorts; comparative (head-to-head) DTA studies; studies nested in trials or surveillance programs.
- Sufficient data to reconstruct a complete 2×2 contingency table (true positives [TP], false positives [FP], false negatives [FN], true negatives [TN]) for FIA and/or ELISA versus the reference standard. Sensitivity, specificity, sample size, and disease prevalence were used to reconstruct tables when cell counts were not reported directly.
- Original primary data. Peer-reviewed articles, conference abstracts with extractable 2×2 data, and preprints (medRxiv/bioRxiv) with sufficient accuracy data were eligible; preprints were flagged for sensitivity analysis.

**Language and date**
- No language restrictions. Non-English articles were translated using certified translation services or bilingual team members.
- No publication-date restriction (databases searched from inception).

### 2.3.2 Exclusion Criteria

- Asymptomatic individuals enrolled solely in seroprevalence or blood-bank screening studies without clinical suspicion of acute dengue.
- Convalescent-phase-only specimens (>7 days after symptom onset) without paired acute-phase specimens.
- Animal studies, in-vitro spiked-sample studies, or analytical-only evaluations without a clinical validation cohort.
- Specimen types other than whole blood, serum, or plasma (e.g., saliva, urine), unless a paired standard specimen was also reported.
- Combined NS1/IgM/IgG assays from which NS1-only results could not be disaggregated.
- Exclusively immunocompromised cohorts (HIV co-infection, transplant recipients) without a general dengue-suspected arm.
- Archived/stored specimens of unknown clinical context with no linked clinical data.
- Narrative reviews, editorials, commentaries, case reports, and case series without denominator data.
- Studies that did not report, and did not permit reconstruction of, a 2×2 table.
- Duplicate publications of the same dataset (the most complete report was retained).

### 2.4.3 Search Concept Structure

The strategy combined five concept blocks (PRISMA-S):

**Block 1 AND Block 2 AND (Block 3 OR Block 4) AND Block 5**

| Block | Concept | Example terms |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | Dengue (target condition) | dengue, DENV, dengue fever, dengue hemorrhagic fever, DENV-1–4 |
| 2 | NS1 antigen (biomarker) | NS1, NS-1, nonstructural protein 1, NS1 antigenemia |
| 3 | Index test (FIA) | fluorescence immunoassay, FIA, TRFIA, immunofluorescence, STANDARD F, SD Biosensor, QUANTI CARD, europium fluorescence |
| 4 | Comparator (ELISA) | ELISA, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, EIA, Platelia, Panbio |
| 5 | Diagnostic accuracy | sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic accuracy, likelihood ratio, ROC, true positive, false negative |

Block 3 OR Block 4 was used so that FIA-only, ELISA-only, and head-to-head studies were all retrieved; FIA-specific and paired data were then isolated at screening. No language, date, or publication-type filters were applied.

## 2.5 Study Selection

Study selection followed a dual-reviewer, independent, two-stage process (PRISMA-DTA items 9–10).

### 2.5.1 Record Management

All records were exported in RIS or BibTeX format, imported into Zotero (version ≥6.0) or EndNote (version ≥21), and deduplicated automatically then verified manually. Unique records were transferred to Rayyan QCRI (https://rayyan.ai) or Covidence for blinded screening.

### 2.5.2 Dual-Reviewer Screening Protocol

1. **Calibration.** Before formal screening, both reviewers independently screened a random sample of 50 titles/abstracts. Discrepancies were discussed to harmonize interpretation of eligibility criteria. Screening proceeded only after Cohen’s κ ≥ 0.80 on the calibration set [12].
2. **Title and abstract screening.** Two reviewers independently screened all unique records against the eligibility criteria. Records marked “unclear” by either reviewer were advanced to full-text review.
3. **Full-text screening.** Two reviewers independently assessed all retrieved full texts. Reasons for exclusion at this stage were recorded using a pre-specified taxonomy (wrong population; wrong index test; no extractable 2×2 data; wrong target condition; duplicate; other).
4. **Consensus mechanism.** Disagreements at either stage were resolved by discussion. If consensus could not be reached, a third reviewer (arbitrator) adjudicated. The arbitrator’s decision was final and documented.
5. **Inter-rater reliability.** Cohen’s κ was calculated for both screening stages and interpreted as: <0.20 poor, 0.21–0.40 fair, 0.41–0.60 moderate, 0.61–0.80 substantial, 0.81–1.00 almost perfect [12].
6. **PRISMA flow diagram.** Counts of records identified, screened, assessed for eligibility, and included — with exclusion reasons at full text — were summarized in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.

Reviewers were not blinded to journal, author, or institution.

## 2.6 Data Collection Process

### 2.6.1 Extraction Form and Piloting

A standardized data extraction form (`data/extraction_template.md`) was developed a priori and piloted independently by both reviewers on the first three included studies. Discrepancies were used to refine field definitions before full extraction.

### 2.6.2 Dual Extraction and Verification

Two reviewers independently extracted data from every included study. Extracted values were compared cell by cell. Discrepancies were resolved by discussion or third-reviewer adjudication. Corresponding authors were contacted by email (one initial request plus one reminder after 14 days) for missing 2×2 data when reconstruction was otherwise impossible. Missing data were recorded as “NR” (not reported) and were not imputed except for the pre-specified continuity correction described in Section 2.8.4.

### 2.6.3 Data Items Extracted

**Study-level:** first author, year, journal, country/region, design, setting, enrollment period, single- vs. multicenter, funding, conflicts of interest.

**Population:** total N; dengue-positive and -negative counts by reference standard; prevalence; age (mean/median, range or IQR); age group (pediatric ≤18 years / adult / mixed); sex (% male); fever duration at collection; WHO 2009 clinical classification [13]; primary vs. secondary infection (n, %; method: IgM/IgG ratio, paired sera, or hemagglutination-inhibition); DENV-1–4 distribution; specimen type and condition (fresh vs. stored).

**Index test (FIA):** commercial name, manufacturer, reader/instrument model, target (NS1 only vs. combination), COI threshold, whether the manufacturer cutoff was used, specimen volume, turnaround time, lot/expiry reporting, operator qualification, blinding, number and handling of indeterminate results.

**Reference standard / comparator:** type (RT-PCR, ELISA, composite, virus isolation); ELISA kit name and manufacturer; RT-PCR target gene and protocol; blinding; same-specimen testing; interval between index and reference; differential verification.

**2×2 tables:** TP, FP, FN, TN for FIA vs. reference; ELISA vs. independent reference (when available); FIA vs. ELISA agreement table for head-to-head studies. Subgroup 2×2 tables were extracted separately when reported (infection status, fever day, serotype, specimen type).

**Derived measures:** sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, PLR, NLR, DOR, Cohen’s κ (extracted if reported; otherwise calculated).

### 2.6.4 Reconstruction of 2×2 Tables

When cell counts were not reported:

- TP = Sensitivity × (number positive by reference standard)
- FN = (1 − Sensitivity) × (number positive by reference standard)
- TN = Specificity × (number negative by reference standard)
- FP = (1 − Specificity) × (number negative by reference standard)

Reconstructed counts were rounded to the nearest integer. Studies from which a complete 2×2 table could not be reconstructed were excluded from quantitative synthesis and described narratively.

## 2.7 Risk of Bias in Individual Studies (QUADAS-2)

Risk of bias and applicability concerns were assessed with the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2) tool [14], tailored to dengue NS1 FIA studies as specified in `analysis/quadas2_protocol.md`. Two reviewers assessed each study independently after a three-study calibration exercise. Disagreements were resolved by discussion or third-reviewer adjudication. Supporting text from each paper was recorded alongside judgments for an audit trail.

### 2.7.1 Domains and Review-Specific Signalling Questions

QUADAS-2 was applied across all four domains. Standard signalling questions were retained; six dengue-specific questions were added (Table 1).

**Table 1. Tailored QUADAS-2 signalling questions**

| Domain | Signalling questions (standard and *review-specific*) |
|--------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **1. Patient selection** | Consecutive or random sample enrolled? Case-control design avoided? Inappropriate exclusions avoided? *Fever duration ≤7 days at specimen collection?* |
| **2. Index test (FIA)** | FIA interpreted without knowledge of the reference standard? Threshold (COI) pre-specified? *Assay performed per manufacturer IFU? Indeterminate/borderline results reported and handled appropriately (<5% excluded)?* |
| **3. Reference standard** | Reference standard likely to classify acute dengue correctly? Interpreted without knowledge of FIA? *Incorporation bias avoided (ELISA not used simultaneously as sole comparator and sole reference)? Reference applied in its diagnostic window (RT-PCR within the viremic period; paired sera for seroconversion)?* |
| **4. Flow and timing** | Appropriate interval (same specimen or ≤24 hours)? All patients received the same reference standard? All patients included in the analysis? *Patient flow described (STARD-style diagram or equivalent)?* |

Each domain was judged **Low / High / Unclear** for risk of bias. Domains 1–3 were additionally judged for **applicability concern** (Low / High / Unclear). Domain 4 has no applicability rating per QUADAS-2.

### 2.7.2 Operational Judgment Rules (Summary)

- **Patient selection — High RoB:** two-gate (case-control) sampling; convenience panels of known positives and negatives; systematic exclusion of severe dengue, secondary infection, or pediatric patients without justification.
- **Index test — High RoB:** FIA interpreted with knowledge of the reference result; post-hoc COI optimization on the study dataset; ≥5% indeterminate results excluded without sensitivity analysis.
- **Reference standard — High RoB:** IgM- or IgG-only ELISA as the sole reference; clear incorporation bias (identical ELISA kit as both comparator and reference); FIA results influencing reference interpretation. NS1 ELISA alone as reference was rated **Unclear** (imperfect reference standard) rather than automatically High.
- **Flow and timing — High RoB:** index and reference performed >24 hours apart; differential verification (e.g., RT-PCR only in FIA-positive patients); >5% of enrolled patients excluded from the 2×2 table without justification.

Applicability was rated High when the enrolled population, FIA platform, or target-condition definition diverged from the review question (e.g., healthy blood-donor controls; prototype/non-commercial FIA; IgG-defined “any past dengue” rather than acute infection).

### 2.7.3 Visualization and Use in Synthesis

Domain-level judgments were tabulated for every study. Traffic-light plots and weighted summary bar charts were generated with the `robvis` R package [15] using `tool = "QUADAS-2"`. Overall RoB (Low vs. High/Unclear) was used in sensitivity analysis (Section 2.10.1) and as a covariate in bivariate meta-regression (Section 2.10.2).

## 2.8 Diagnostic Accuracy Measures and Statistical Synthesis

### 2.8.1 Unit of Analysis and Primary Outcomes

The unit of analysis was the study (or a mutually exclusive subgroup 2×2 table when a study reported independent tables by infection status, fever day, or serotype). The primary outcomes were:

1. Pooled **sensitivity** (Se)
2. Pooled **specificity** (Sp)
3. **Positive likelihood ratio** (PLR = Se / [1 − Sp])
4. **Negative likelihood ratio** (NLR = [1 − Se] / Sp)
5. **Diagnostic odds ratio** (DOR = [Se / (1 − Se)] / [(1 − Sp) / Sp])
6. Hierarchical summary ROC (**HSROC**) curve with area under the curve (AUC), 95% confidence region, and 95% prediction region

Secondary outcomes were: head-to-head FIA vs. ELISA accuracy on paired specimens; Cohen’s κ; narrative synthesis of turnaround time and COI–severity correlations.

### 2.8.2 Descriptive Synthesis

Study characteristics, population descriptors, FIA and ELISA platforms, reference standards, and individual-study Se/Sp with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were summarized in tables. Coupled forest plots of sensitivity and specificity were produced for visual inspection of between-study variability using `mada::forest()` and custom `ggplot2` figures [16].

### 2.8.3 Primary Model: Bivariate Random-Effects GLMM (Reitsma)

The primary quantitative synthesis used the **bivariate random-effects generalized linear mixed model** of Reitsma et al. [17], implemented in the R package `mada` (`reitsma()`) [16]. This model jointly estimates logit-transformed sensitivity and logit-transformed false-positive rate (FPR = 1 − specificity) as a bivariate normal pair:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\text{logit}(\text{Se}_i) \\
\text{logit}(\text{FPR}_i)
\end{pmatrix}
\sim
\mathcal{N}
\left(
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_{\text{Se}} \\
\mu_{\text{FPR}}
\end{pmatrix},
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
\right)
\]

where \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\) is an unstructured between-study variance–covariance matrix with variances \(\tau^2_{\text{Se}}\) and \(\tau^2_{\text{FPR}}\) and covariance \(\rho \cdot \tau_{\text{Se}} \cdot \tau_{\text{FPR}}\). The model:

- Accounts for the negative correlation between sensitivity and specificity induced by threshold variation
- Incorporates binomial within-study sampling error
- Yields a summary operating point (pooled Se and Sp) with 95% CIs

Variance components were estimated by **restricted maximum likelihood (REML)** as the default. Maximum likelihood (ML) was used in a sensitivity analysis (Section 2.10.1). Derived measures (PLR, NLR, DOR) and their 95% CIs were obtained by parametric bootstrap (10,000 draws from the multivariate normal distribution of the fixed-effect coefficients using the model variance–covariance matrix) [16,17].

### 2.8.4 Hierarchical Summary ROC (HSROC)

Harbord et al. demonstrated that, in the absence of covariates, the bivariate Reitsma model is mathematically equivalent to the **HSROC** model of Rutter and Gatsonis [18,19]. Both parameterizations were reported. The HSROC curve was plotted with:

- The summary operating point
- The 95% confidence region around the summary point
- The 95% prediction region (between-study variability)
- Individual study estimates in ROC space (symbol size proportional to sample size)

HSROC parameters were extracted from the `reitsma` summary: **Λ** (accuracy), **Θ** (threshold), and **β** (shape/symmetry). AUC was reported as a global accuracy summary.

### 2.8.5 Zero-Cell Correction

Studies with a zero count in any cell of the 2×2 table received a continuity correction of **0.5** added to all four cells, the default in `mada` (`correction = 0.5`, `correction.control = "all"`) and standard Cochrane DTA practice [5,16]. Alternative corrections (0.1 and 1.0) were examined in sensitivity analysis.

### 2.8.6 Head-to-Head FIA versus ELISA

For the subset of studies that tested the **same specimens** with both FIA and ELISA against an independent reference (or that reported a direct FIA–ELISA cross-classification):

- Separate bivariate models were fitted for FIA and ELISA on the overlapping study set
- Summary operating points and HSROC curves were overlaid
- Agreement (overall percent agreement, Cohen’s κ, McNemar’s test) was synthesized when 2×2 FIA-vs-ELISA tables were available

A minimum of four studies was required for a bivariate head-to-head model.

### 2.8.7 Publication Bias

Publication bias was assessed with **Deeks’ funnel plot asymmetry test**, the recommended method for DTA meta-analyses [20]. Effective sample size (ESS) was defined as:

\[
\text{ESS} = \frac{4 \times n_{\text{pos}} \times n_{\text{neg}}}{n_{\text{pos}} + n_{\text{neg}}}
\]

where \(n_{\text{pos}} = \text{TP} + \text{FN}\) and \(n_{\text{neg}} = \text{FP} + \text{TN}\). Log diagnostic odds ratio (with 0.5 continuity correction) was regressed on \(1/\sqrt{\text{ESS}}\). A two-sided *p* < 0.10 on the slope coefficient was interpreted as evidence of potential small-study effects / publication bias. A funnel plot of \(1/\sqrt{\text{ESS}}\) versus ln(DOR) was presented.

### 2.8.8 Software

Analyses were performed in **R** (R Core Team, version ≥4.3.0) [21] using:

- `mada` (≥0.5.12) — bivariate Reitsma model, HSROC, forest plots [16]
- `metafor` — univariate random-effects models for heterogeneity (Cochran’s Q, I², τ²) [22]
- `meta` — supporting meta-analytic utilities
- `mvmeta` — multivariate meta-regression backend used by `mada`
- `robvis` — QUADAS-2 visualization [15]
- `ggplot2` / `gridExtra` — publication graphics

The analysis script is archived at `analysis/dta_meta_analysis.R`. Session information will be reported for reproducibility.

## 2.9 Investigation of Heterogeneity and Subgroup Analyses

### 2.9.1 Quantification of Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity was assessed by:

1. **Visual inspection** of coupled forest plots and the HSROC scatter in ROC space
2. **Cochran’s Q** for logit-sensitivity and logit-specificity (α = 0.10, given the low power of the test) [23]
3. **I²** with interpretation: 0–40% might not be important; 30–60% moderate; 50–90% substantial; 75–100% considerable [23]
4. Bivariate variance components \(\tau^2_{\text{Se}}\) and \(\tau^2_{\text{FPR}}\) from \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\)
5. Between-study correlation **ρ** between logit-Se and logit-FPR; ρ < −0.3 was interpreted as consistent with a threshold effect

Univariate Q and I² were computed with `metafor::rma()` on logit-transformed Se and Sp using inverse-variance weights. They are reported as descriptive supplements to the bivariate τ² estimates; they do not replace the bivariate model as the primary inference.

### 2.9.2 Pre-Specified Subgroup Analyses

If I² > 50% or Q-test *p* < 0.10, or regardless as pre-specified clinically important strata, subgroup analyses were conducted when **≥4 studies** contributed data to each stratum. Separate bivariate models were fitted within each subgroup.

| Subgroup | Categories | Clinical / methodological rationale |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------------|
| Infection status | Primary vs. secondary | Secondary infection reduces free NS1 via immune-complex formation, lowering sensitivity [24] |
| Fever duration | Early acute (days 0–3) vs. late acute (days 4–7) | NS1 peaks days 1–4 and declines as IgM/IgG rise [25] |
| DENV serotype | DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, DENV-4 | Serotype-specific NS1 secretion; DENV-1 often higher than DENV-2 [26] |
| Reference standard | RT-PCR vs. ELISA vs. composite | Imperfect reference and incorporation bias can distort Se/Sp |
| FIA platform | STANDARD F (SD Biosensor) vs. other FIA | Manufacturer-specific analytical performance |
| Geographic region | Southeast Asia vs. South Asia vs. Latin America vs. other | Differing serotype mix, secondary-infection prevalence, and healthcare pathways |
| Age group | Pediatric (≤18 years) vs. adult (>18 years) | Age-dependent immune response and NS1 antigenemia |
| Study design | Prospective vs. retrospective | Spectrum and verification bias more likely in retrospective series |

Studies reporting only mixed (unstratified) data were excluded from that subgroup contrast but retained in the overall analysis. “Mixed” was not treated as a third analytic category.

## 2.10 Sensitivity Analyses and Meta-Regression

### 2.10.1 Pre-Specified Sensitivity Analyses

The following analyses tested robustness of the pooled operating point:

1. **Exclusion of high risk-of-bias studies.** Re-analysis after excluding studies judged High RoB in ≥2 QUADAS-2 domains.
2. **Restriction to a robust reference standard.** Analysis limited to studies using RT-PCR or virus isolation (Tiers 1–2), excluding ELISA-only reference standards.
3. **Leave-one-out influence analysis.** Sequential omission of each study; changes in pooled Se, Sp, and DOR were tabulated to identify influential studies.
4. **Exclusion of preprints and conference abstracts.** Peer-reviewed full-text publications only.
5. **Alternative continuity correction.** Corrections of 0.1 and 1.0 versus the default 0.5.
6. **ML versus REML.** Comparison of pooled Se and Sp under maximum likelihood versus restricted maximum likelihood variance estimation.

### 2.10.2 Bivariate Meta-Regression

Sources of heterogeneity were further examined with **bivariate meta-regression**, adding study-level covariates to the Reitsma model:

```r
reitsma(data, formula = cbind(tsens, tfpr) ~ covariate,
        correction = 0.5, method = "reml")
```

Pre-specified covariates:

- Reference standard type (RT-PCR vs. ELISA vs. composite)
- FIA commercial platform/manufacturer
- Specimen type (whole blood vs. serum/plasma)
- Study design (prospective vs. retrospective)
- Dengue prevalence in the study population (≤50% vs. >50%)
- Geographic region (Southeast Asia, South Asia, Latin America, other)
- Fever-duration category (early / late / mixed)
- Infection-type mix (primary-predominant / secondary-predominant / mixed)
- Overall QUADAS-2 RoB (Low vs. High/Unclear)

Covariates were entered one at a time (univariable meta-regression) because the expected number of studies is modest relative to the number of parameters in a multivariable bivariate model. Nested models (covariate vs. intercept-only) fitted by ML were compared with a likelihood-ratio test; *p* < 0.05 was considered evidence that the covariate explained residual heterogeneity. Regression coefficients on the logit-Se and logit-FPR scales were exponentiated and back-transformed to differences in sensitivity and specificity for clinical interpretation.

Meta-regression was performed only when the covariate had ≥2 levels and each level contained sufficient studies for model identifiability (typically ≥4 per level for categorical factors). Continuous covariates (prevalence, mean fever day), if reported uniformly, were modelled linearly on the logit scale.

### 2.10.3 Certainty of the Evidence (Optional Supplementary)

If the evidence base permitted, certainty of pooled sensitivity and specificity was graded using GRADE for DTA reviews (risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias) [27]. GRADE Summary of Findings tables will be provided as supplementary material when applicable.
