## SECTION 14: REVIEW QUESTION

### 14.1 Structured Review Question (PIRD Framework)

This systematic review and meta-analysis addresses the following question using the Population, Index test, Reference standard, Diagnosis of interest (PIRD) framework for diagnostic test accuracy (DTA) reviews:

> **"What is the diagnostic accuracy of fluorescence immunoassay (FIA) compared with enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for the detection of dengue virus non-structural protein 1 (NS1) antigen in patients with suspected acute dengue infection?"**

| PIRD Component    | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Population**    | Patients of any age with clinically suspected acute dengue infection (fever duration ≤7 days from symptom onset), presenting to healthcare facilities in dengue-endemic or epidemic regions. |
| **Index Test**    | Fluorescence immunoassay (FIA) for dengue NS1 antigen detection (any commercially available platform, e.g., STANDARD F Dengue NS1 Ag FIA [SD Biosensor], or equivalent FIA-based kits).    |
| **Reference Standard** | Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for dengue NS1 antigen (e.g., Platelia Dengue NS1 Ag ELISA [Bio-Rad], Panbio Dengue Early ELISA [Abbott], or equivalent); and/or reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for dengue RNA as a composite or standalone reference standard. |
| **Diagnosis (Target Condition)** | Acute dengue virus infection (DENV serotypes 1–4) as confirmed by NS1 antigenemia.                                                                                       |

### 14.2 Secondary Review Questions

1. Does the diagnostic accuracy of FIA for dengue NS1 differ between primary and secondary dengue infections?
2. Does the diagnostic accuracy of FIA for dengue NS1 vary by DENV serotype (DENV-1 through DENV-4)?
3. Is the diagnostic accuracy of FIA for dengue NS1 influenced by the timing of specimen collection relative to fever onset (early acute phase: days 0–3 vs. late acute phase: days 4–7)?
4. Does the commercial platform/manufacturer of FIA influence diagnostic accuracy estimates?

## SECTION 17: CONDITION OR DOMAIN BEING STUDIED

**Dengue virus infection** — an acute febrile illness caused by dengue virus (DENV), a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus of the family *Flaviviridae*, genus *Flavivirus*. Four antigenically distinct serotypes exist (DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, DENV-4), all transmitted to humans primarily by *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus* mosquitoes.

Dengue is the most rapidly spreading mosquito-borne viral disease globally, with an estimated 390 million infections per year (Bhatt et al., 2013, *Nature*, 496:504–507, doi:10.1038/nature12060), of which approximately 96 million manifest clinically. The World Health Organization (WHO) classifies dengue into dengue (with or without warning signs) and severe dengue, replacing the older DHF/DSS classification (WHO, 2009).

**NS1 antigen** is a highly conserved 46–50 kDa non-structural glycoprotein secreted by DENV-infected cells at high concentrations (up to 50 µg/mL in serum during the acute viremic phase). NS1 is detectable from the first day of symptom onset through approximately day 9, making it a key biomarker for early laboratory confirmation of dengue before the appearance of IgM antibodies (typically day 4–5) (Muller et al., 2017, *J Infect Dis*, 215:S13–S17, doi:10.1093/infdis/jiw649).

## SECTION 18: PARTICIPANTS/POPULATION

### Inclusion Criteria — Population
- Patients of **any age** (neonates, children, adolescents, adults) with clinically suspected acute dengue infection.
- Fever duration **≤7 days** from symptom onset at the time of specimen collection.
- Presentation to any healthcare setting (inpatient, outpatient, emergency department, primary care, or sentinel surveillance site) in **dengue-endemic or epidemic regions**.
- Specimens: venous whole blood, serum, or plasma.

### Exclusion Criteria — Population
- Asymptomatic individuals screened in seroprevalence or blood-bank screening studies (no clinical suspicion of acute dengue).
- Convalescent-phase only specimens (collected >7 days after symptom onset without paired acute-phase specimens).
- Animal or in-vitro spiked-sample studies without clinical validation cohorts.
- Studies using specimen types other than whole blood, serum, or plasma (e.g., saliva, urine) unless paired with a standard specimen type.

## SECTION 20: INDEX TESTS

**Index Test: Fluorescence Immunoassay (FIA) for Dengue NS1 Antigen**

FIA is a rapid diagnostic technology that uses fluorescent-labeled antibodies and an optical reader to detect dengue NS1 antigen in clinical specimens. Unlike conventional immunochromatographic tests (ICTs) that rely on subjective visual interpretation of colored lines, FIA provides objective, reader-based quantification via Cutoff Index (COI) values, reducing inter-operator variability.

Commercially available FIA platforms for dengue NS1 include but are not limited to:
- **STANDARD F Dengue NS1 Ag FIA** (SD Biosensor Inc., Suwon, South Korea)
- **STANDARD F200 Analyzer**-compatible cartridges
- **Sofia Dengue NS1** (Quidel Corporation) — if FIA-based
- Other fluorescence-based lateral flow or immunochromatographic platforms with quantitative readout

**Test characteristics:**
- Specimen type: whole blood, serum, or plasma (100 µL)
- Turnaround time: 5–15 minutes
- Readout: automated/semi-automated via fluorescence reader (objective, quantitative COI)
- Threshold for positivity: as defined by each manufacturer's instructions for use (IFU)

All FIA test results will be interpreted using the manufacturer-recommended cutoff. Studies using modified or non-standard cutoffs will be flagged and addressed in sensitivity analyses.

## SECTION 21: COMPARATOR(S)/REFERENCE STANDARD(S)

### 21.1 Primary Comparator Test

**Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) for Dengue NS1 Antigen**

ELISA is a laboratory-based immunoassay considered the standard method for dengue NS1 antigen detection in well-equipped diagnostic laboratories. Commercially available NS1 ELISA kits include:
- **Platelia Dengue NS1 Ag ELISA** (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Marnes-la-Coquette, France)
- **Panbio Dengue Early ELISA** (Abbott Diagnostics, now Alere/Abbott)
- **STANDARD E Dengue NS1 Ag ELISA** (SD Biosensor Inc.)
- **InBios DENV Detect NS1 ELISA** (InBios International, Seattle, USA)

ELISA serves as both **comparator test** (for head-to-head accuracy comparison with FIA) and as a **reference standard** in studies where RT-PCR is unavailable.

### 21.2 Reference Standard Hierarchy

The following reference standard hierarchy will be applied to classify studies:

| Tier | Reference Standard                            | Classification                |
|------|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1    | RT-PCR (real-time or conventional) for DENV RNA | Gold standard                 |
| 2    | Virus isolation (cell culture) ± RT-PCR         | Gold standard                 |
| 3    | Composite reference standard (RT-PCR + paired IgM/IgG seroconversion + NS1 ELISA) | Acceptable reference |
| 4    | NS1 ELISA alone (when RT-PCR is unavailable)    | Acceptable with notation      |

Studies will be classified by their reference standard tier, and **sensitivity analysis by reference standard type** will be performed to evaluate the impact of imperfect reference standards (incorporation bias and verification bias).

## SECTION 23: OTHER INCLUSION/EXCLUSION CRITERIA

### Additional Inclusion Criteria
1. **2×2 contingency table extractability**: Studies must report sufficient data (sensitivity, specificity, TP, FP, FN, TN, or raw counts) to reconstruct a complete 2×2 table for FIA and/or ELISA against the reference standard.
2. **Original data**: Studies must present original, primary diagnostic accuracy data (not secondary analyses of previously published datasets, unless new 2×2 data are derivable).
3. **Publication type**: Peer-reviewed journal articles and published conference abstracts with sufficient diagnostic accuracy data. Preprints from medRxiv/bioRxiv will be included if sufficient data are reported and will be flagged in sensitivity analyses.

### Additional Exclusion Criteria
1. Studies where NS1 detection is performed as part of a combined NS1/IgM/IgG test and results for NS1 alone cannot be disaggregated.
2. Studies enrolling exclusively immunocompromised populations (e.g., HIV-coinfected, organ transplant recipients) without a general dengue-suspected cohort arm.
3. Studies conducted entirely on archived/stored specimens of unknown clinical context (no linked clinical data).
4. Non-human studies.

## SECTION 24: CONTEXT

This review is situated within the context of increasing global dengue burden and the urgent need for rapid, accurate, point-of-care diagnostics in resource-limited settings. The WHO Global Strategy for Dengue Prevention and Control (2012–2020, extended) emphasizes integrated surveillance and early diagnosis as cornerstones of dengue management (WHO, 2012).

While NS1 ELISA remains a highly accurate laboratory-based diagnostic method, its requirement for specialized equipment (microplate readers, incubators), trained personnel, and 3–5 hour turnaround times limits its utility in field settings, rural clinics, and during outbreak surges. FIA platforms offer a potential bridge between the low accuracy of subjective visual ICTs and the high accuracy but slow throughput of ELISA, by providing reader-based, objective quantification with a 15-minute turnaround.

Existing meta-analyses (Defined Health et al., 2020, *Cad Saúde Pública*, 36:e00225618; Defined Health et al., 2022, *Int J Environ Res Public Health*, 19:8756) have focused on ICT-based rapid tests broadly, but **no systematic review has specifically evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of FIA platforms for dengue NS1 against ELISA as both comparator and reference standard**. This evidence gap is the primary rationale for the present review.

## SECTION 25: PRIMARY OUTCOME(S)

The primary outcomes of this systematic review are the diagnostic accuracy parameters of FIA for dengue NS1 antigen detection, measured against the defined reference standard:

1. **Pooled Sensitivity** (Se) — proportion of true dengue-positive cases correctly identified by FIA, with 95% confidence interval (CI).
2. **Pooled Specificity** (Sp) — proportion of true dengue-negative cases correctly identified by FIA, with 95% CI.
3. **Positive Likelihood Ratio (PLR)** — the ratio of the probability of a positive FIA result in dengue-positive patients to the probability of a positive FIA result in dengue-negative patients.
4. **Negative Likelihood Ratio (NLR)** — the ratio of the probability of a negative FIA result in dengue-positive patients to the probability of a negative FIA result in dengue-negative patients.
5. **Diagnostic Odds Ratio (DOR)** — the ratio of the odds of a positive test result in dengue-positive patients to the odds of a positive test result in dengue-negative patients.
6. **Summary Receiver Operating Characteristic (SROC) Curve** — with area under the curve (AUC) and 95% confidence/prediction regions.

---

## SECTION 26: SECONDARY OUTCOME(S)

1. **Head-to-head comparison of pooled sensitivity and specificity of FIA versus ELISA** for dengue NS1 detection (in the subset of studies evaluating both tests on the same specimens).
2. **Agreement between FIA and ELISA** (Cohen's kappa coefficient with 95% CI, where reported).
3. **Correlation between FIA Cutoff Index (COI) values and disease severity markers** (e.g., platelet count, hematocrit), where reported.
4. **Turnaround time comparison** between FIA and ELISA (narrative synthesis).

## SECTION 27: DATA EXTRACTION (SELECTION AND CODING)

### 27.1 Study Selection Process

Study selection will follow PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-DTA guidelines:

1. **Deduplication**: All retrieved records will be imported into a reference management tool (Zotero or EndNote) and deduplicated automatically followed by manual verification.
2. **Title and abstract screening**: Two reviewers will independently screen all unique records against the eligibility criteria. Screening will be performed using Rayyan QCRI (https://rayyan.ai) or Covidence systematic review software.
3. **Full-text screening**: Records passing title/abstract screening will be retrieved in full text. Two reviewers will independently assess full-text articles for eligibility. Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be documented.
4. **Disagreement resolution**: Discrepancies between reviewers at any stage will be resolved by discussion. If consensus cannot be reached, a third reviewer (arbitrator) will adjudicate.
5. **Inter-rater reliability**: Cohen's kappa (κ) will be calculated for both screening stages to quantify inter-reviewer agreement. Kappa values will be interpreted as: <0.20 = poor, 0.21–0.40 = fair, 0.41–0.60 = moderate, 0.61–0.80 = substantial, 0.81–1.00 = almost perfect (Landis & Koch, 1977, *Biometrics*, 33:159–174).

### 27.2 Data Extraction

A standardized, piloted data extraction form will be used. The following data will be extracted from each included study:

**Study-level data:**
- First author, publication year, journal, country, study design
- Study setting (hospital-based, community-based, sentinel surveillance)
- Enrollment period, sample size, dengue prevalence in the study population
- Funding source and declared conflicts of interest

**Population-level data:**
- Age distribution (mean/median, range or IQR)
- Sex distribution (% male)
- Fever duration at specimen collection (days from symptom onset)
- Clinical classification (dengue fever, dengue with warning signs, severe dengue per WHO 2009 criteria)
- Primary versus secondary infection status (as determined by IgM/IgG ratio or paired sera)
- DENV serotype distribution (where available)
- Specimen type (whole blood, serum, plasma)

**Index test data (FIA):**
- Commercial name and manufacturer of FIA kit
- FIA platform/reader model
- Cutoff Index (COI) threshold for positivity
- Lot number and expiry (if reported)
- Operator qualification

**Reference standard data:**
- Type of reference standard used (RT-PCR, ELISA, composite)
- Commercial name and manufacturer of ELISA kit (if applicable)
- RT-PCR target gene and protocol (if applicable)
- Blinding: whether index test and reference standard were interpreted independently

**2×2 contingency table data:**
- True Positives (TP), False Positives (FP), False Negatives (FN), True Negatives (TN)
- If not directly reported: sensitivity, specificity, total tested, and prevalence will be used to reconstruct the 2×2 table
- Zero-cell corrections: a continuity correction of 0.5 will be added to all cells of studies with zero counts in any cell (standard Cochrane DTA practice)

**Derived accuracy measures (extracted or calculated):**
- Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV, NPV
- PLR, NLR, DOR
- Cohen's kappa (if reported)

## SECTION 29: STRATEGY FOR DATA SYNTHESIS

### 29.1 Descriptive Synthesis

A narrative and tabular summary of all included studies will be presented, including study characteristics, population demographics, FIA and ELISA platforms used, reference standards applied, and individual study sensitivity/specificity estimates.

**Coupled forest plots** of sensitivity and specificity (with 95% CIs) will be generated for visual inspection of between-study variability.

### 29.2 Statistical Modeling

#### 29.2.1 Primary Analysis: Bivariate Random-Effects Model

The primary meta-analysis will use the **bivariate generalized linear mixed model (GLMM)**, also known as the Reitsma model (Reitsma et al., 2005, *J Clin Epidemiol*, 58:982–990, doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2005.02.022). This model:
- Jointly models pairs of logit-transformed sensitivity and false-positive rate (1 − specificity) across studies.
- Accounts for between-study heterogeneity via random effects with an unstructured variance-covariance matrix.
- Accounts for the negative correlation between sensitivity and specificity that arises from threshold variation across studies.
- Produces pooled estimates of sensitivity, specificity, PLR, NLR, and DOR with 95% CIs.

Harbord et al. (2007, *Biostatistics*, 8:239–251, doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxl004) demonstrated that the bivariate model is mathematically equivalent to the **Hierarchical Summary ROC (HSROC)** model of Rutter & Gatsonis (2001, *Stat Med*, 20:2865–2884, doi:10.1002/sim.942) in the absence of covariates. Both parameterizations will be reported.

#### 29.2.2 HSROC Curve Analysis

A **Hierarchical Summary Receiver Operating Characteristic (HSROC) curve** will be constructed, displaying:
- The summary operating point (pooled sensitivity and specificity)
- The 95% confidence region around the summary point
- The 95% prediction region (reflecting between-study variability)
- Individual study estimates plotted in ROC space

The HSROC parameters (Λ = accuracy, Θ = threshold, β = symmetry) will be reported when the HSROC parameterization is applied.

#### 29.2.3 Variance Estimation

Variance components will be estimated using **restricted maximum likelihood (REML)** as the default method. Maximum likelihood (ML) estimation will be used as a sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of variance estimates.

#### 29.2.4 Zero-Cell Correction

For studies with zero counts in any cell of the 2×2 table, a continuity correction of **0.5** will be added to all four cells prior to analysis.

### 29.3 Assessment of Heterogeneity

Between-study heterogeneity will be assessed using:
1. **Visual inspection** of forest plots and SROC plots for spread of study estimates.
2. **Cochran's Q test** for homogeneity (significance level α = 0.10, given low power of the test).
3. **I² statistic** with interpretation thresholds: 0–40% = might not be important; 30–60% = moderate; 50–90% = substantial; 75–100% = considerable (Higgins et al., 2003, *BMJ*, 327:557–560, doi:10.1136/bmj.327.7414.557).
4. **Variance parameters** (τ² for logit-sensitivity and logit-FPR) from the bivariate model, with 95% CIs.
5. **Correlation coefficient (ρ)** between logit-transformed sensitivity and logit-transformed FPR from the bivariate model, to assess threshold effect.

### 29.4 Investigation of Heterogeneity Sources

If significant heterogeneity is detected (I² > 50% or Q-test p < 0.10), the following pre-specified sources of heterogeneity will be investigated:

#### Meta-Regression (Bivariate Model with Covariates)
Using the formula interface of the `reitsma()` function: `cbind(tsens, tfpr) ~ covariate`, the following covariates will be examined:
- Reference standard type (RT-PCR vs. ELISA vs. composite)
- FIA commercial platform/manufacturer
- Specimen type (whole blood vs. serum/plasma)
- Study design (prospective vs. retrospective)
- Dengue prevalence in the study population (≤50% vs. >50%)
- Geographic region (Southeast Asia, South Asia, Latin America, other)
- QUADAS-2 overall risk of bias (low vs. high/unclear)

### 29.5 Publication Bias Assessment

- **Deeks' funnel plot asymmetry test** (Deeks et al., 2005, *J Clin Epidemiol*, 58:882–893, doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2005.01.016) — the recommended test for publication bias in DTA meta-analyses. A p-value < 0.10 will be considered indicative of potential publication bias.
- Visual inspection of a funnel plot of 1/sqrt(effective sample size) versus diagnostic log odds ratio.

### 29.6 Software

All statistical analyses will be performed using **R** (R Core Team, version ≥4.3.0) with the following packages:
- `mada` (version ≥0.5.12) — bivariate Reitsma model, SROC curves, forest plots (Doebler, 2020)
- `meta` and `metafor` — supporting meta-analysis functions
- `mvmeta` — multivariate meta-analysis

## SECTION 30: ANALYSIS OF SUBGROUPS OR SUBSETS

The following pre-specified subgroup analyses will be conducted, contingent on a sufficient number of studies (≥4 per subgroup) reporting the relevant data:

### 30.1 Pre-Specified Subgroups

| Subgroup Variable | Categories | Rationale |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Infection status** | Primary vs. Secondary dengue infection | Secondary infections may have lower NS1 antigenemia due to pre-existing anti-NS1 antibodies from prior heterotypic infection, reducing assay sensitivity (Duong et al., 2011, *PLoS Negl Trop Dis*, 5:e1199, doi:10.1371/journal.pntd.0001199). |
| **Timing of specimen collection** | Early acute phase (days 0–3 post-fever onset) vs. Late acute phase (days 4–7) | NS1 antigen concentrations peak early and decline as IgM/IgG antibodies rise, potentially affecting both FIA and ELISA sensitivity differentially (Muller et al., 2017). |
| **DENV serotype** | DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, DENV-4 | Serotype-specific variation in NS1 secretion levels has been documented, with DENV-1 and DENV-2 generally associated with higher NS1 levels than DENV-3 and DENV-4 in some populations. |
| **Reference standard type** | RT-PCR vs. ELISA vs. composite | To assess the impact of imperfect reference standards on accuracy estimates (differential verification bias). |
| **FIA commercial platform** | STANDARD F (SD Biosensor) vs. other FIA platforms | To assess manufacturer-specific performance variation. |
| **Geographic region** | Southeast Asia vs. South Asia vs. Latin America vs. other | To assess generalizability across endemic settings with differing epidemiological contexts. |
| **Age group** | Pediatric (≤18 years) vs. Adult (>18 years) | To evaluate age-dependent differences in immune response and NS1 antigenemia. |
| **Study design** | Prospective vs. Retrospective | To assess potential spectrum bias from retrospective designs. |

### 30.2 Sensitivity Analyses

The following pre-specified sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the robustness of pooled estimates:

1. **Exclusion of high risk-of-bias studies**: Re-analysis after excluding studies judged as high risk of bias in ≥2 QUADAS-2 domains.
2. **Exclusion of studies with imperfect reference standards**: Re-analysis restricted to studies using RT-PCR or virus isolation as the reference standard.
3. **Influence analysis**: Leave-one-out analysis to identify influential studies that disproportionately affect pooled estimates.
4. **Exclusion of preprints and conference abstracts**: Restricting to peer-reviewed full-text publications only.
5. **Alternative continuity correction**: Using correction values of 0.1 and 1.0 (instead of 0.5) for studies with zero cells to assess impact on pooled estimates.
6. **ML vs. REML estimation**: Comparing pooled estimates under maximum likelihood and restricted maximum likelihood variance estimation.

## SECTION 38: DETAILS OF EXISTING REVIEW(S) ON THE SAME TOPIC

The following existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses address related but distinct questions:

1. **Defined Health et al. (2020).** Rapid immunochromatographic tests for the diagnosis of dengue: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Cad Saúde Pública*, 36(6):e00225618. doi:10.1590/0102-311X00225618. PROSPERO: CRD42014009885.

   - *Scope:* Evaluated ICT-based NS1 and IgM tests; did not include FIA as a separate technology category.

3. **Defined Health et al. (2024).** Dengue: A review of laboratory diagnostics in the vaccine age. *J Med Microbiol*, 73:001833. doi:10.1099/jmm.0.001833.
   - *Scope:* Narrative review of dengue diagnostics including RDTs, ELISAs, and NAATs; not a formal DTA meta-analysis.

**Evidence gap:** No existing systematic review or meta-analysis has specifically evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of FIA-based platforms for dengue NS1 antigen detection against ELISA as comparator and/or reference standard. This review addresses that gap.
